Korean Hangul: letters and sounds (Language)

Introduction
Hangul is the alphabet used to write Korean. It was created in 1443 under King Sejong the Great and announced in 1446. Before Hangul, Koreans mostly wrote with Chinese characters, which were hard to learn.

Hangul has 14 basic consonants and 10 basic vowels, so 24 letters in all. The letters are not written in a line like English. They are stacked into square syllable blocks.

Basic consonants
- ㄱ sounds like g, as in go.
- ㄴ sounds like n.
- ㄷ sounds like d.
- ㄹ sounds like a light r or l.
- ㅁ sounds like m.
- ㅂ sounds like b.
- ㅅ sounds like s.
- ㅈ sounds like j.
- ㅎ sounds like h.
- ㅇ is silent at the start of a syllable.
Aspirated consonants
- ㅋ sounds like k, with a puff of breath.
- ㅌ sounds like t, with a puff of breath.
- ㅍ sounds like p, with a puff of breath.
- ㅊ sounds like ch, with a puff of breath.

Basic vowels
- ㅏ sounds like a, as in father.
- ㅓ sounds like eo, like the u in cup.
- ㅗ sounds like o.
- ㅜ sounds like u, as in moon.
- ㅡ sounds like eu, a flat sound with the lips spread.
- ㅣ sounds like i, as in see.
- ㅑ, ㅕ, ㅛ and ㅠ add a y sound: ya, yeo, yo and yu.

Building syllable blocks
- A syllable block starts with a consonant followed by a vowel.
- A vertical vowel such as ㅏ or ㅣ goes to the right of the consonant.
- A horizontal vowel such as ㅗ, ㅜ or ㅡ goes below the consonant.
- Example: 가 is ㄱ plus ㅏ, and sounds like ga.
- Example: 나 is ㄴ plus ㅏ, and sounds like na.
- Example: 고 is ㄱ plus ㅗ, and sounds like go.
- Example: 구 is ㄱ plus ㅜ, and sounds like gu.

The silent ㅇ and double consonants
- When a syllable begins with a vowel sound, the silent ㅇ is written first: 아 sounds like a and 오 sounds like o.
- At the end of a syllable, ㅇ sounds like ng.
- Double consonants are pronounced tighter and stronger: ㄲ, ㄸ, ㅃ, ㅆ and ㅉ.

Reading simple words
- 나무 (namu) means tree.
- 바다 (bada) means sea.
- 고기 (gogi) means meat.
- 아기 (agi) means baby.
- 사랑 (sarang) means love.
- 안녕 (annyeong) is a greeting that means hello.
