Acids, Bases and Salts (Chemistry)

Introduction
Acids, bases and salts are three important types of chemical substances that are commonly found in everyday life.

They have different properties and can be identified using indicators and the pH scale.

Acids
Acids are substances that release hydrogen ions (H⁺) when dissolved in water.

Properties of Acids:
- Generally have a sour taste.
- Turn blue litmus paper red.
- Have a pH value below 7.
- React with certain metals to release hydrogen gas.
- React with bases to form salt and water.
Examples:
- Hydrochloric acid (HCl)
- Sulphuric acid (H₂SO₄)
- Acetic acid (CH₃COOH)
- Citric acid (found in lemons and oranges).

Bases
Bases are substances that neutralise acids. Many bases release hydroxide ions (OH⁻) when dissolved in water.

Properties of Bases:
- Generally have a bitter taste.
- Feel slippery or soapy.
- Turn red litmus paper blue.
- Have a pH value above 7.
- React with acids to form salt and water.
Examples:
- Sodium hydroxide (NaOH)
- Potassium hydroxide (KOH)
- Calcium hydroxide (Ca(OH)₂)
- Magnesium hydroxide (Mg(OH)₂).

Salts
Salts are ionic compounds generally formed when an acid reacts with a base in a neutralisation reaction.

Properties of Salts:
- Usually crystalline solids.
- Many salts dissolve in water.
- Can have different colours and properties.
- Some salts conduct electricity when dissolved in water or melted.
Examples:
- Sodium chloride (NaCl): Common table salt.
- Copper sulphate (CuSO₄): Used in laboratories and agriculture.
- Calcium carbonate (CaCO₃): Found in limestone and chalk.

pH Scale
The pH scale measures how acidic or basic a solution is. It generally ranges from 0 to 14.
- Below 7: Acidic (example: lemon juice)
- 7: Neutral (example: pure water)
- Above 7: Basic (example: soap solution)
A lower pH indicates a stronger acidic nature, while a higher pH indicates a stronger basic nature.

Indicators
Indicators are substances used to identify whether a solution is acidic or basic.

Common indicators include:
1. Litmus: Turns red in acids and blue in bases.
2. Phenolphthalein: Colourless in acids and pink in bases.
3. Methyl Orange: Red in acids and yellow in bases.
4. Turmeric: Remains yellow in acids but turns reddish-brown in bases.

Neutralisation Reaction
Neutralisation is a chemical reaction in which an acid reacts with a base to produce salt and water.

General Equation: Acid + Base → Salt + Water
Example: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
Hydrochloric acid + Sodium hydroxide → Sodium chloride + Water

Applications in Everyday Life
1. Agriculture: Lime is added to acidic soil to reduce its acidity.
2. Medicine: Antacids help neutralise excess stomach acid.
3. Food: Acids such as citric acid and acetic acid are used in food preparation and preservation.
4. Cleaning: Bases are used in soaps and cleaning products.
5. Industry: Acids and bases are widely used in chemical manufacturing and processing.
