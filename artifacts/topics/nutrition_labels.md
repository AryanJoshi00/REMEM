Nutrition Labels: how to read food packaging (Health)

Introduction
A nutrition label tells you what is in packaged food. Reading it helps you compare products and understand how much of each nutrient you are actually eating.

Serving size
- The numbers on a label are given for one serving, or for a fixed amount such as 100 grams.
- A pack often contains more than one serving, so eating the whole pack means multiplying the numbers.
- Always check the serving size and the number of servings per pack before comparing products.

Calories and energy
- Energy in food is measured in calories, written as kcal, or in kilojoules, written as kJ.
- One kilocalorie equals about 4.2 kilojoules.
- Calories tell you how much energy the food gives you, not how healthy it is.

Macronutrients
- The three macronutrients are carbohydrates, protein and fat.
- Carbohydrates give 4 kcal per gram.
- Protein gives 4 kcal per gram.
- Fat gives 9 kcal per gram, more than double the other two.
- Fibre is a carbohydrate that the body cannot fully digest, and it helps digestion.

Sugars
- Total sugars include sugars that occur naturally, such as in fruit and milk.
- Added sugars are sugars put in during making, such as in biscuits and soft drinks.
- Many health guidelines advise limiting added sugars.

Sodium and salt
- Sodium is the part of salt that affects blood pressure when eaten in large amounts.
- Salt contains about 40 percent sodium.
- To convert sodium to salt, multiply the sodium amount by 2.5.

Ingredients and daily value
- Ingredients are listed in order of weight, with the heaviest ingredient first.
- If sugar appears near the top of the list, the food contains a lot of sugar.
- Some labels show the percent daily value, which tells how much one serving adds to a typical day's needs.
- A percent daily value of 5 percent or less is low, and 20 percent or more is high.
