Photosynthesis (Biology)

Introduction
Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants prepare their own food using sunlight, carbon dioxide and water. It mainly takes place in the leaves of plants.

During this process, light energy is converted into chemical energy, and oxygen is released as a by-product.

Requirements for Photosynthesis
1. Sunlight: Provides the energy needed for photosynthesis.
2. Chlorophyll: Green pigment that absorbs sunlight.
3. Carbon Dioxide: Obtained from the atmosphere through tiny pores called stomata.
4. Water: Absorbed by roots from the soil and transported to the leaves.

Chemical Equation
6CO2 + 6H2O --(Sunlight)--> C6H12O6 + 6O2
Carbon dioxide + Water → Glucose + Oxygen

Site of Photosynthesis
Photosynthesis takes place inside chloroplasts, which are present in plant cells.
- Chloroplast: Contains chlorophyll and is the main site of photosynthesis.
- Thylakoids: Structures where light-dependent reactions occur.
- Grana: Stacks of thylakoids.
- Stroma: Fluid-filled region where the Calvin cycle occurs.

Stages of Photosynthesis
1. Light-Dependent Reaction
- Takes place in the thylakoid membranes.
- Requires sunlight.
- Chlorophyll absorbs light energy.
- Water molecules are split through a process called photolysis.
- Oxygen is released.
- ATP and NADPH are produced.
2. Light-Independent Reaction (Calvin Cycle)
- Takes place in the stroma.
- Does not directly require sunlight.
- Uses ATP and NADPH produced in the first stage.
- Carbon dioxide is converted into organic molecules.
- These molecules are eventually used to produce carbohydrates.

Factors Affecting Photosynthesis
- Light Intensity: Higher light intensity generally increases the rate of photosynthesis.
- Carbon Dioxide: Increased CO2 availability increases the rate up to a certain limit.
- Temperature: Photosynthesis increases with temperature up to an optimum level.
- Water: Lack of water reduces the rate of photosynthesis.
- Chlorophyll: Less chlorophyll reduces the absorption of light.

Importance of Photosynthesis
1. Produces food for plants.
2. Releases oxygen into the atmosphere.
3. Absorbs carbon dioxide.
4. Converts solar energy into chemical energy.
5. Supports food chains and maintains ecological balance.
