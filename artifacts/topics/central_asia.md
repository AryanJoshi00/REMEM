Central Asia: five countries and their facts (Geography)

Introduction
Central Asia is a region of five countries that were once part of the Soviet Union: Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and Turkmenistan. Their names end in -stan, which comes from a Persian word meaning land of. The region lies on the old Silk Road trade routes between China and Europe.

Kazakhstan
- The capital is Astana.
- Kazakhstan is the largest landlocked country in the world by area.
- The main languages are Kazakh and Russian.
- The currency is the tenge.
- Much of the country is dry grassland called steppe.

Uzbekistan
- The capital is Tashkent.
- Uzbekistan is doubly landlocked: it is surrounded only by other landlocked countries.
- The ancient Silk Road cities of Samarkand and Bukhara are in Uzbekistan.
- The language is Uzbek and the currency is the som.
- Part of the shrinking Aral Sea lies in Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan.

Kyrgyzstan
- The capital is Bishkek.
- Most of the country is covered by mountains.
- Issyk-Kul is a large mountain lake that does not freeze in winter.
- The language is Kyrgyz and the currency is the som.

Tajikistan
- The capital is Dushanbe.
- More than 90 percent of the country is mountainous, including the Pamir Mountains.
- The Tajik language is closely related to Persian.
- The currency is the somoni.

Turkmenistan
- The capital is Ashgabat.
- The Karakum Desert covers about 70 percent of the country.
- The language is Turkmen and the currency is the manat.
- The Darvaza gas crater, nicknamed the Gates of Hell, has been burning since the 1970s.
