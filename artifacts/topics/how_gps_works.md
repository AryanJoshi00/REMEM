How GPS Works: finding a place from space (Technology)

Introduction
GPS stands for Global Positioning System. It was built and is run by the United States. A GPS receiver, such as the one in a phone, works out where it is by listening to radio signals from satellites. The receiver only listens; it never sends anything to the satellites.

The satellites
- The GPS constellation has at least 24 satellites in six orbital planes.
- The satellites orbit about 20,200 kilometres above the Earth.
- Each satellite circles the Earth about twice a day.
- Each satellite carries very accurate atomic clocks.
- A satellite continuously broadcasts its position and the exact time the signal was sent.

Measuring distance with time
- Radio signals travel at the speed of light.
- The receiver notes when a signal arrives and compares it with the time the signal was sent.
- Distance equals the speed of light multiplied by the travel time.
- A signal from a GPS satellite takes roughly 0.07 seconds to reach the ground.

Trilateration
- Trilateration finds a position from its distances to several known points.
- The distance to one satellite places the receiver somewhere on a sphere around that satellite.
- Two satellites narrow the position down to a circle where the two spheres meet.
- Three satellites narrow it down to two points, and one of them is usually far out in space.

Why four satellites are needed
- A receiver has a cheap clock that is not as accurate as an atomic clock.
- A small clock error becomes a large distance error, because light travels about 300 000 kilometres every second.
- A fourth satellite lets the receiver solve for its own clock error as well as its three position coordinates.
- This is why a receiver needs signals from at least four satellites.

Relativity and corrections
- Einstein's theory of relativity affects the satellite clocks.
- The net effect of their speed and weaker gravity is that the satellite clocks run faster by about 38 microseconds per day compared with clocks on the ground.
- Without correcting this, GPS positions would drift by several kilometres each day.
- The satellite clocks are adjusted so that the correction is built in.

Other systems and accuracy
- Other global systems are GLONASS from Russia, Galileo from the European Union and BeiDou from China.
- NavIC is India's regional navigation system.
- A typical smartphone GPS is accurate to within about 5 metres in open sky.
- Tall buildings and tunnels block or reflect signals and reduce accuracy.
