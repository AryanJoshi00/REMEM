Photography Basics: the exposure triangle (Photography)

Introduction
Exposure is the amount of light that reaches the camera sensor. Too little light makes a dark photo and too much makes a bright, washed-out one. Three settings control exposure: aperture, shutter speed and ISO. Together they are called the exposure triangle.

Aperture
- Aperture is the size of the opening in the lens that lets light in.
- It is measured in f-numbers such as f/1.8, f/4 and f/16.
- A smaller f-number means a wider opening, so more light enters.
- A wide aperture gives a shallow depth of field: the subject is sharp and the background is blurred.
- A narrow aperture gives a deep depth of field: both near and far objects are sharp.

Shutter speed
- Shutter speed is how long the sensor is exposed to light.
- It is measured in seconds or fractions of a second, such as 1/1000 or 1/30.
- A fast shutter speed freezes motion, as in a sports photo.
- A slow shutter speed lets more light in but blurs anything that moves.
- Holding a camera by hand at a slow shutter speed causes blur from camera shake. A common guide is to use a speed of at least 1 over the focal length.

ISO
- ISO is the sensitivity of the sensor to light.
- A low ISO such as 100 gives a clean image but needs plenty of light.
- A high ISO such as 3200 makes the photo brighter in the dark but adds noise, which looks like grain.

Stops
- A stop is a doubling or halving of the amount of light.
- Typical aperture stops are f/1.4, f/2, f/2.8, f/4, f/5.6, f/8, f/11 and f/16.
- Opening the aperture by one stop lets in twice as much light.
- Doubling the ISO from 200 to 400 makes the image one stop brighter.
- You can trade settings: a faster shutter speed can be balanced by a wider aperture or a higher ISO.

Composition and light
- The rule of thirds places the subject along the lines that divide the frame into three equal parts, or at their crossings.
- Golden hour is the hour after sunrise and the hour before sunset, when the light is soft and warm.
- White balance adjusts colours so that white objects look white under different lighting.
